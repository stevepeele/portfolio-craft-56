02 / Console

Processing and Control

The console is where captured sources and playback returns become two different products: the mixes the band plays inside, and the mix the audience hears. Every routing and gain decision made here is felt in both.

Depends on

Capture, for clean sources at consistent gain. Playback, returning as inputs that must sit inside the live arrangement. Production design, for the show file, scenes, and patch decided in advance.

Feeds

Monitoring, with the per musician mixes built here. House, with the main mix, system output, and headroom the room is heard through.

  • 1Gain structure. Clean, consistent gain with headroom preserved at every stage.
  • 2Routing and bussing. A path that keeps house and monitors independent.
  • 3Mix architecture. Layers that hold together and adapt quickly in the show.
  • 4Scene programming. Snapshots labeled and documented, recalled without surprise.
  • 5Processing judgment. Equalization and dynamics that serve intelligibility first.
  • 6Show control. Calm operation under show conditions, changes made early and small.

On its own: console and show file work can be engaged separately, from building a show file for your engineer to running the desk for a show or run.

I build and run the systems that carry a performance from the first input to the last seat in the room. House, monitoring, playback, production design, and deployable systems are each a discipline in their own right, and each one is practiced here to the same standard.

What connects them is systems thinking: knowing how a decision in one facet changes the others. That understanding travels with whichever facet a production engages, whether that is one facet on its own or several together. What follows is each facet in depth, and the connections between them.

Base: Cincinnati and Loveland, Ohio Work: Single shows, runs, and tour preparation Focus: Live bands and live production

SEC. A

My Universe

The systems map

A show is a connected system. Change the monitor mix and you change how the singer phrases. Change the playback architecture and you change how the band breathes. Retune the room and every decision at the desk that follows it changes with it.

My Universe is that system, mapped as it is actually worked: capture, console, monitoring, playback, and house as connected layers, held inside production design. The map at the top of this page is the working version of it. Select any layer to see what it depends on, what it feeds, and the constellation of competencies inside it. A production does not have to engage all of it. Whichever layer is engaged is handled with the whole system in view.

How the work flows

The same cycle, every engagement, whatever the facet. The map above is the system. This is the order it is worked in.

  1. W.01DesignAdvance, plots, system and show design. Decisions are made while they are still cheap to change.Production design leads. All layers planned.
  2. W.02BuildRig assembled, patched, labeled, and tested. Playback built redundant. Show file programmed.Capture, console, playback, systems.
  3. W.03SoundcheckRoom read and tuned, sources gained, monitor mixes built per musician, playback checked against the band.Capture, monitoring, house, playback.
  4. W.04ShowThe performance protected in real time: mixes held, cues delivered, problems corrected before the audience hears them.All layers, live.
  5. W.05StrikeDocumented teardown, faults logged, show file and notes updated so the next date starts from what was learned.Documentation back into design.

05 / HouseFOH and Room

Depends on: the console mix, playback sitting inside it, and a room read honestly before doors. Feeds: the audience. It is the layer every other layer is ultimately judged through.

Competencies: room assessment, system tuning and alignment, gain structure, vocal intelligibility, low frequency management, translation checks from every listening position.

On its own: front of house for a single show, a run, or a room that needs a steady hand.

00 / Production DesignThe Frame

Depends on: accurate advance information and the artist's intent. Governs: every layer. It sets the patch, the show file, the playback structure, the monitor plan, and the house approach before load in, so show day is execution, not invention.

Competencies: technical advance, stage and system design, show programming, coordination across departments, documentation, consistency across venues.

On its own: design and programming ahead of a show or tour, handed to the team that will run it.

Listening Notes

What I listen for

Listening is the discipline underneath all the others. Systems and equipment matter only insofar as they serve what can actually be heard, in that room, on that night.

  • L.01The room before the mixI listen to the room empty, then as it fills. Its reflections, its low frequency build up, the places where speech loses clarity. The mix is built for that room, on that day, not recalled from the last one.
  • L.02The vocal, intelligible at any levelIf the words cannot be understood, the performance has not reached the audience. Everything else is placed around the vocal, preserving presence without forcing level.
  • L.03Low frequency with intentionKick, bass, and playback low end have to occupy separate, defined places. Power down there comes from clarity, not from accumulation.
  • L.04TranslationA mix is only finished when it holds at the desk, at the back wall, in the front rows, and in the monitors on stage. I check it from every position the band and the audience will hear it from.
CONSTELLATION 01

House Engineering

Front of house

Mixes that translate, and judgment that holds under show conditions.

Front of house is a series of decisions made in real time, in a room that keeps changing as people arrive, as the band settles, and as the night builds. The discipline is not knowing which control to move. It is knowing what the room will need ten seconds from now, and making the smaller, earlier correction instead of the larger, later one.

I mix for intelligibility first, then for impact. Headroom is protected throughout, so the moments that should lift are able to. When the work is done well, the audience does not notice the engineering at all. They notice the band.

Competencies

Numbered to the constellation above
  • 1Room assessmentReading size, surfaces, occupancy, and coverage before a note is played, and adjusting the plan to what is actually there.
  • 2System tuning and alignmentSetting level, equalization, and delay so the system itself disappears and the music is what remains.
  • 3Gain structureClean, consistent gain from input to output, with headroom preserved at every stage of the path.
  • 4Mix architectureBuilding a mix in layers that hold together and can be adapted quickly without losing their centre.
  • 5Feedback and dynamics controlAnticipating problems before the audience hears them, and managing dynamics without flattening the performance.
  • 6Show judgmentKnowing when to push, when to pull back, and when to leave a good mix alone.

Engaged on its ownHouse engineering can be booked by itself: front of house for a single show, a run, or a tour, managed end to end within that facet, from soundcheck to the last song.

CONSTELLATION 02

Monitoring

In-ear systems

Monitoring that protects the performance.

A monitor mix is not a smaller house mix. It is the environment the musician plays inside. If it is harsh, crowded, or unpredictable, the performance tightens around it. If it is clear and stable, musicians stop thinking about the system and play.

My responsibility is that stability, night after night, and the level discipline that protects hearing across a long run of shows. A mix that is trusted does not need to be loud. It needs to be right, and to stay right.

Competencies

Numbered to the constellation above
  • 1Mixes built per musicianEach mix shaped around what that player needs in order to perform, not a copy of another mix with the level changed.
  • 2RF disciplineCoordinated frequencies, checked range, and clean antenna practice, so wireless systems are never left to chance.
  • 3Signal integritySplits and routing that keep monitor and house independent, so a change in one does not compromise the other.
  • 4Level and hearing disciplineMixes that remain clear at responsible levels, sustained across a full set and a full tour.
  • 5Soundcheck methodA repeatable process that gets every mix close quickly, then refines it from the musician's language, not the engineer's.
  • 6Communication on stageQuiet, precise talkback and cueing that supports the show without ever becoming part of it.

Engaged on its ownMonitoring can be booked by itself: a monitor mix and in ear system run for your show or run, whether or not I am at front of house.

CONSTELLATION 03

Playback

Tracks, click, and cues

Playback that cannot fail, and does not ask to be noticed.

Playback carries click, cues, and the parts of the arrangement that cannot be played live. When it works, the audience never considers it. When it fails, everyone does. There is no middle state, so it is engineered accordingly.

I treat playback as a performance system in its own right: structured, rehearsed, redundant, and integrated with the band so closely that the boundary between what is played and what is played back stops being the point. The show continues even if a component does not. That is planned in advance, not improvised under pressure.

Competencies

Numbered to the constellation above
  • 1Session architectureSessions built in a consistent, legible structure that another engineer can open, understand, and run.
  • 2Redundancy and failoverA second path, tested in advance, with a changeover that can be executed calmly in the middle of a show.
  • 3Click, cues, and count insClear, musical guidance for the band, voiced and timed so it supports phrasing rather than policing it.
  • 4Integration with the live bandLevels, tuning, and timing aligned in rehearsal, so playback sits inside the performance instead of on top of it.
  • 5Change managementEdits, set changes, and rearrangements made without destabilising a show file that already works.
  • 6Rehearsal and show disciplineThe same checks, in the same order, every date, until reliability is a habit rather than a hope.

Engaged on its ownPlayback can be booked by itself: designed, built, and operated for a production that already has its house and monitor engineers in place.

CONSTELLATION 04

Production Design and Programming

Design and show programming

Production design that serves the artist and the room.

Design is the work done before load in that determines whether load in is calm. Programming is how that design is made repeatable. Together they mean the show the artist intended is the show that happens, in a club, a theatre, or a room that was never built for concerts.

Good design also respects scale. It sizes the system to the room and the crew available to run it, decides what travels and what is sourced locally, and leaves nothing important to be invented on show day. The result is not spectacle for its own sake. It is a production that fits.

Competencies

Numbered to the constellation above
  • 1Technical advanceAccurate information exchanged early: plots, lists, power, and constraints, with no surprises left for show day.
  • 2Stage and system designLayout and system choices sized to the room, the programme, and the crew available to run them.
  • 3Show programmingScenes, snapshots, and cues programmed, labeled, and documented so the show runs as designed, every night.
  • 4Coordination across departmentsAudio planned alongside stage, lighting, and backline as one production, rather than competing setups.
  • 5DocumentationPlots, input lists, and show notes written clearly enough to be used under pressure, by someone else.
  • 6Consistency across venuesA core show that adapts to each room without being rebuilt from the beginning each time.

Engaged on its ownProduction design and programming can be booked by themselves: the design, advance, and show programming done ahead of the date, and handed to the team that will run it.

CONSTELLATION 05

Deployable Systems and Deployment

Builds and touring deployment

Deployable systems that any engineer can walk up to and run.

Anyone can assemble equipment. The work is designing a system that travels, deploys quickly, fails gracefully, and reads clearly to an engineer who did not build it. Labeling, documentation, and logical layout are not finishing touches. They are the design. If a system needs its builder standing beside it to explain it, it is not finished.

On tour, that discipline compounds. Fast changeovers, consistent patch, serviceable wiring, and a show file that behaves the same in every room give the band the one thing touring rarely offers: a stable technical foundation to perform from. Systems are built, programmed, and tested before the run, and handed over with the plots and notes the work actually requires.

Competencies

Numbered to the constellation above
  • 1System architectureThe whole signal path designed as one system, from stage input to house output and monitor split.
  • 2Wiring, labeling, and documentationEvery cable, port, and path identified, so a fault can be traced in minutes rather than guessed at.
  • 3Power and groundingClean, safe power distribution and grounding practice that prevents noise and protects equipment and people.
  • 4Patch and changeover speedLayouts that allow a full band to be patched, checked, and ready within the time the schedule actually allows.
  • 5Roadworthiness and serviceabilityBuilds that withstand transport, can be serviced in the field, and do not depend on fragile workarounds.
  • 6HandoverSystems delivered with the plots, notes, and walkthrough another engineer needs to run the show without me.

Engaged on its ownA rig build can be booked by itself: a deployable system designed, built, programmed, and documented for your band, or for your own engineer to run.

Spec note: a recent deployable build centers on a StudioLive 16R with Helix Rack processing, integrating mixing, processing, and playback in a single rack footprint for full band use.
